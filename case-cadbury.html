Cadbury × SRK

Campaign deconstruction · Diwali 2021. Independent case study, not client work by Sagar Sharma.

A brand gave away its most expensive asset to 85,000 small shops — and got back more than any ad could have bought.

  • +32%: Sales vs −30% expected
  • 85,000+: Stores given free ads
  • $1.5M: Earned PR value

The One-Sentence Summary

Cadbury gave Shah Rukh Khan's endorsement away to 85,000+ local businesses — using AI to make him name each store personally.

The Business Reality

Diwali is roughly 45% of Cadbury Celebrations' annual sales. In 2020–21 the category was down sharply, gifting was expected to fall 30%, and the small retailers who drive 80%+ of Celebrations sales were struggling.

The Tension Most Analyses Miss

Cadbury's gift packs compete with local mithai shops. Consumers wanted to support local. Rather than hide from that conflict, the campaign resolved it in public: yes, we're a big company — and an ally of the ecosystem we sell through.

The Inverse Celebrity Logic

In the store-specific versions SRK never mentions Cadbury. He talks about the local shop. That takes brand confidence — trusting people to remember who enabled the generosity without being told.

The Results

What I Took From It

  • 01: Tension is opportunity.
  • 02: Sometimes the best use of a celebrity is not using them for yourself.
  • 03: Technology serves emotion — the "how", never the "what".
  • 04: Purpose has to be operational, not stated.

How I contributed

Wrote the campaign deconstruction: context, insight hierarchy, creative strategy, technology, execution, results and five lessons.

What I learned

The best use of a brand asset can be giving it away. Generosity worked because it was operational — not just messaging.
