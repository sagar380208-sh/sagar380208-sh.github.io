Blinkit

Growth-marketing ROI · Same spend, different shape. Independent case study, not client work by Sagar Sharma.

Hold the ₹500 Cr marketing budget flat, move paid acquisition into retention, and free about ₹1,200 Cr of GMV — a modelled growth-marketing case for India's quick-commerce leader.

  • ~₹1,200 Cr: GMV freed at the same budget (modelled)
  • +8 pp: 90-day repeat rate (modelled)
  • −29%: Blended CAC (modelled)

Independent case study — not work for Blinkit or Eternal. I diagnosed the funnel, estimated unit economics where the company does not disclose them (and said so on the slide), designed the reallocation, two A/B tests and a 12-month roadmap. Costs and lifts are working estimates anchored to public benchmarks.

The Brief

Blinkit leads Indian quick commerce at about 48% category share and turned Adjusted EBITDA positive in Q4 FY26 (+₹37 Cr, 0.3% of NOV). But ad spend rose 163% year on year to ₹502 Cr in FY25 while net loss widened to ₹930 Cr, and management itself named "poor-quality growth" as the headline risk. The question: where does the next ₹500 Cr of marketing go so that Blinkit keeps growing fast while protecting its bridge to a 5–6% steady-state margin?

Where the Leak Is

The leak isn't at the top of the funnel. It's between order 2 and order 5.

About 47% of new users churn before their fifth order — the moment usage becomes a habit. On an indexed funnel of 100 aware users, 38 install and 22 order, but only about 6.7 are still repeating at 90 days. Blinkit does not disclose this funnel, so I estimated it from public and benchmark data and labelled it that way. WhatsApp win-back reactivates 25–35% of lapsed users, against 8–12% for email.

Channel Economics

Paid install isn't broken — retention channels are simply about three times better per rupee. Verdicts by channel: CRM and WhatsApp, referral, and tier-2 creators — scale; paid social and paid search — hold; offline and out-of-home — cut.

Same Spend, Different Shape

Roughly 35% of paid acquisition moves into CRM, referral and tier-2 creator launches while the total budget stays flat. The principle: a reactivated user avoids the cost of buying the same slot again. Modelled on a baseline of about ₹2 lakh Cr annualised GMV, with a ±25% sensitivity band.

Three Levers

  • WhatsApp lifecycle nudges between orders 2 and 5: coupon ladders, free delivery auto-applied above ₹149, recipe-of-the-day nudges. Expected lift of 5–8 pp for about ₹35 Cr.
  • A 25-city creator-led launch wave: 10–20 micro-creators per city in a four-week burst, timed to a hyperlocal dark-store launch. About ₹70 Cr for roughly 0.9M extra orders a month.
  • A referral-wallet revamp with a halo tier: single-tier ₹75 becomes three tiers of ₹50 / ₹100 / ₹200 tied to the third and sixth order. About +1.5 pp for ₹20 Cr.

Proving It Before Scaling

Guardrails are written in before launch: unsubscribe rate at or below 1.5%, net order value not eroded by more than 5 pp, and any lever that drags contribution per order is paused within 14 days. An eight-tile dashboard tracks funnel conversion, CAC and LTV by channel, cohort retention and test results.

Modelled Net Uplift

Same marketing spend, twelve months: +8 pp on 90-day repeat, +1.7M monthly transacting users, about ₹1,200 Cr of freed GMV and a 29% lower blended CAC.

Roadmap, Kill Criteria and Decisions

Kill criterion at week 16: 90-day repeat under +3 pp, or blended CAC not below ₹100. Four risks are named — a discount war from rivals, regulatory pressure on "10-minute" framing, order-value erosion in tier-2, and dark-store compliance at scale. Three decisions are asked of leadership this month: approve the reallocation into CRM and WhatsApp within 60 days, greenlight the creator wave, and cut out-of-home spend once Test 01 reads at week 8.

  • Months 0–2: Fix the leak: stand up the four-flow WhatsApp journey, instrument first-five-order cohorts, run Test 01.
  • Months 2–4: Reallocate: move spend into CRM and creators, launch the referral halo tier, run Test 02.
  • Months 4–12: Scale winners: roll Test 02 into the 25-city wave and sunset offline spend.

How I contributed

Independent case study. I diagnosed the funnel, estimated unit economics (and marked the estimates), designed the budget reallocation and the two A/B tests, and wrote the roadmap and kill criteria.

What I learned

More acquisition spend is rarely the answer when the leak is in retention. When a company doesn't disclose the data, the honest move is to triangulate, bound each figure and show the range — and to design a test that proves the idea before scaling it.
