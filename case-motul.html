Motul India

5-year strategic expansion. Independent case study, not client work by Sagar Sharma.

A premium specialist in a market defended by distribution moats and price wars. Don't fight for the mass — own the specialism.

  • ₹1,800–2,500 Cr: Revenue target
  • 18–22%: Target CAGR
  • ~₹350 Cr: Planned capex

The Market Read

In a $7B+ lubricants market, the barriers to mass entry are structural: distribution moats, price wars and OEM lock-ins. Attacking them head-on destroys capital for a premium brand.

The Positioning Choice

Don't compete for the mass market. Own the specialisms the incumbents can't serve profitably.

Premium synthetics, EV fluids and high-performance two-wheelers — where technical credibility beats distribution scale.

Five-Pillar Growth Roadmap

  • 01: Rural hub-and-spoke expansion.
  • 02: A D2C digital ecosystem.
  • 03: EV product innovation.
  • 04: Subscriptions with CRM-driven retention.
  • 05: A content-led acquisition funnel.

Financial & Risk Framework

Scenario-based projections with ~₹350 Cr capex and a profitability path, plus mitigation for pricing pressure, supply-chain volatility and counterfeits — the underrated threat to premium lubricants in India.

How I contributed

Independent case study. I analysed the lubricants market and its barriers, set the specialist positioning, and built the five-pillar roadmap, scenario model and risk framework.

What I learned

In a market defined by distribution moats and price wars, specialism — premium synthetics, EV fluids, performance two-wheelers — is a more defensible route than scale.
