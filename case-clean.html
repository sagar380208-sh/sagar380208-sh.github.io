The Premise

Don't ask for altruism. Offer status — and let the algorithm do the recruiting.

A behavioural-economics framework to operationalise cleanliness through the creator economy. Creators clean because it builds their personal brand, not only because it's good.

Six Psychological Drivers

The Cyclic Value Loop

Viral math (target): 100,000 seed views × 1.5% share rate → 1,500 shares; 0.2% creator conversion → 200 new posts; ~4,000,000 new views at a 20K average.

  • 1: Creator films hook → clean → proof → reveal, tags location.
  • 2: Viewers engage — guilt or pride trigger; saves and shares.
  • 3: Velocity pushes content to non-followers.
  • 4: New creators copy the format; ward rivalry kicks in.
  • 5: More spots cleaned; local shops donate supplies.
  • 6: Leaderboards and shout-outs validate status. Repeat.

Content & Platform Protocol

  • Standard formats (20–45s): Glow-Up Clean (hero), Drain-Run Test (visual ASMR), the ₹99 Kit Challenge (accessibility), Tag Your Ward (rivalry).
  • Mandatory for trust: geo-tag and timestamp, a visible disposal end-point, PPE on camera.
  • Strictly prohibited: faces of litterers, medical waste on camera, political symbols.
  • Platform roles: Instagram for community, YouTube Shorts for search and scale, LinkedIn for CSR legitimacy.

Scaling — Ward-Level Cells

Each ward runs a micro-cell — a Ward Leader with a Creator, Fixer, Logger and Recruiter — equipped with a "brand-in-a-box" kit. A monthly Ward Cup league turns participation into rivalry; inter-RWA and school leagues extend it.

  • P0: 2 weeks — brand kit, overlay templates, 20 pilot creators.
  • P1: 4 weeks — 3 wards/city, 300 posts, loop metric R ≥ 1.2.
  • P2: 8–12 weeks — 30 wards, launch the Ward Cup, local media.
  • P3: Q3–Q4 — 20 cities, state dashboards, inter-city challenges.
