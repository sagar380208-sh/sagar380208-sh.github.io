Ather Energy

Tier-2/3 expansion strategy · Electric two-wheelers. Independent case study, not client work by Sagar Sharma.

How a premium EV brand opens India's Tier-2/3 cities without diluting what it is worth in the metros. A board-style expansion case, modelled at ₹4,800 Cr of incremental FY31 revenue.

  • ₹4,800 Cr: Modelled incremental FY31 revenue
  • ₹4,200 Cr: Base-case NPV at 14% (modelled)
  • 1,800–2,000: Stores by FY31, from 700

Independent case study — not work for Ather Energy. I sized the market two ways, designed the entry playbook, built the bear / base / bull financial case and wrote the board decision card. Market and company figures are public; the forecasts are my models.

The Question

Can Ather extend the franchise downstream without unravelling what the brand is worth in metros?

Ather built a premium metro franchise — 700 stores and 548 service centres, clustered in the South, with Rest-of-India share under 10%. Bajaj's sub-₹1L Chetak and TVS's dealer density now press on that frontier. The case frames two paths: stay in the metros and plateau near ₹8,000 Cr, or push into Tier-2/3 and reach about ₹15,000 Cr by FY31.

Sizing the Prize

Two independent builds of the Tier-2/3 pool land at ₹38,750 Cr and ₹24,500 Cr. I anchored the model on the lower one.

The top-down build multiplies India's 2W base by EV penetration, Tier-2/3 share and blended ASP. The bottom-up build counts 95 named cities plus a semi-urban belt. The ~37% gap is because top-down counts every Tier-2/3 EV sale, while bottom-up counts only cities Ather's three-tier network can plausibly serve. Cross-check: NITI Aayog puts Tier-2 and Tier-3 at about 55% of 2W EV sales by FY28.

The Store Plan

National share moves from about 14% to about 22% by FY31, and the share has to migrate out of the South. Adding 18 points of Rest-of-India share takes roughly four times the units of defending an extra 5 points in the South.

Four Plays, One Rule

  • Product and price: a five-rung ladder tuned to income tier — Konarc ₹1.0L, Rizta S ₹1.21L, Rizta Z+ ₹1.45L, 450X ₹1.65L, 450 Apex ₹1.95L. The shared rule: segment the product before segmenting the geography.
  • Charging: densify Ather Grid by tier, from 6,000+ chargers today to a target of 18,000 by FY31 — one charger per 35 vehicles in Tier-1, per 70 in Tier-2 hubs, per 130 in Tier-3.
  • Service: a hub-and-satellite model — one full-service hub per Tier-2 anchor city (about ₹3 Cr capex) with 30+ partner-run satellites (about ₹50L each), against Bajaj's 3,800+ touchpoints.
  • Financing: pooled NBFC loans targeting about 7.5% all-in against 10% today, an Ather insurance bundle, and a Battery-as-a-Service pilot in FY28 that cuts entry price by about 30%.

The Financial Case

The base case takes Ather from ₹3,823 Cr in FY26 to about ₹15,000 Cr by FY31: IRR of 32%, payback of 3.8 years, a 14% discount rate and a conservative terminal value (7× EBITDA, 2% growth). All figures are modelled.

Five Ways It Fails

  • Competitor matches price (high likelihood, high impact): sign exclusive NBFC and battery-swap bundles in the top 30 Tier-2 cities first, rather than reacting.
  • Subsidy phase-out: hedge with the BaaS pilot and build to 30% LFP cells by FY30.
  • Service-network cost overruns: capex-light satellites with fee-per-service; close any city under 50% utilisation after six months.
  • Brand dilution from the mass play: show Konarc and Rizta in dedicated Studios, not flagship Experience Centres; audit the brand quarterly.
  • Cash burn before FY28 break-even: phase capex and raise ₹700–900 Cr on an EBITDA-positive read.

The Decision: Go, With Phasing

Segment the geography together with the product — don't take the brand downstream by accident.

Three kill criteria: South metro share down more than 400 bps over four consecutive quarters; under six months of opex in cash after Q1 FY28 with no line of sight to EBITDA; or a sub-₹90k rival SKU reaching 30% of national 2W EV share. The ask to the board: approve the three-tier playbook, a ₹250–350 Cr capex envelope over FY27–28, and a quarterly review against those criteria.

  • 100 days: Pick 30 Tier-2 cities, lock partners, anchor one NBFC at about 7.5%.
  • Year 1: Reach 700 → 1,200 stores; pilot Battery-as-a-Service in five cities.
  • Year 3: 2,000 stores, about 22% national share, about ₹15,000 Cr revenue.

How I contributed

Independent case study. I did the market sizing (top-down and bottom-up, reconciled), the store and share plan, the four plays, the three-scenario financial model, the risk register and the board decision card.

What I learned

Expansion is a product problem before it is a geography problem. A cheaper scooter in a new city is the easy part; keeping the premium brand intact in the cities you already own is what decides whether the plan is worth doing. And a good plan states when to stop.
