Independent case study, not Google work. My strongest product work. I personally conducted the 200+ user interviews and ran three-week diary studies, then designed the architecture and prototypes.

Problem Discovery

Intention doesn't equal execution. 67% of users feel unproductive daily, rely on an average of 4.2 apps, and use their calendar only for meetings (80%). The planning layer exists; nothing closes the loop behind it.

Research

  • 200+: User interviews, conducted personally.
  • 3 wks: Diary studies — longitudinal, not one-off.
  • 3: Competitors mapped: RescueTime, Forest, Toggl Track.

The Insight

Users don't need a planner. They need a feedback loop.

No product integrates time tracking, focus, reflection and calendar in one place. Each solves a quarter of the problem — which is why people run four apps and still feel unproductive.

Plan → Do → Reflect

  • Plan: Smart Time Tracker — auto-tracks usage via Android and Chrome, comparing planned against actual.
  • Do: Focus Sessions — calendar blocks become deep-work sessions with DND and Pomodoro.
  • Reflect: Productivity Journal — a daily reflection with AI-generated insights.

Technical Architecture

Privacy Strategy

On-device processing, only aggregated data synced, user-controlled privacy levels. In a category built on surveillance, privacy is the differentiator — not a compliance checkbox.

Prototypes Built

A calendar heatmap of planned vs actual, a daily dashboard, an AI assistant panel, and a mobile reflection UI.

How I contributed

I personally conducted the 200+ user interviews and the three-week diary studies, then defined the Plan → Do → Reflect architecture, the privacy model and the prototypes.

What I learned

Users don't need another planner — they need a feedback loop. Diary studies showed what people actually do, which is rarely what they say in an interview.
