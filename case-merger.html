Eternal × Blinkit

Post-merger integration · A 12-month playbook. Independent case study, not client work by Sagar Sharma.

Four years after a ₹4,447 Cr all-stock deal, how does Blinkit turn a scale lead into a margin moat? A 12-month integration playbook, written top-down in the Minto pyramid style.

  • ₹4,447 Cr: 2022 all-stock deal value
  • 2,443 → ~3,000: Dark stores in 12 months
  • ₹4,000–5,000 Cr: Modelled Adj. EBITDA run-rate

Independent case study — not work for Eternal. I framed the single governing thought, built the three supporting arguments, sized the value-capture ledger and risks, and designed the four-phase roadmap and KPI dashboard. Savings ranges are illustrative estimates, and the deck says so.

The Question

Blinkit's lead is contestable, not yet defensible.

In Q1 FY27 Blinkit's Adjusted EBITDA turned positive at +0.6% of NOV (+₹102 Cr) on NOV of ₹17,132 Cr, up 86% year on year, with 2,443 dark stores. The question: over the next 12 months, which four workstreams, in what sequence, take Blinkit towards the high end of a 5–6% steady-state margin while defending share against a tougher, well-funded field? Capital is not the constraint (₹18,857 Cr of cash). Execution quality, per-store productivity outside the metros and inventory-led regulatory exposure are.

The Governing Thought

Use the next 12 months to convert scale into a margin moat — closing the path to 5–6% Adj. EBITDA on a ₹1.4–1.6 lakh Cr NOV base while stepping the network to about 3,000 dark stores.

  • Scale has compounded: 377 dark stores in FY23 to 2,443 now; NOV up about 3.1× in 24 months; first place in 8 of the top 10 cities.
  • Margin has inflected: five quarters from −1.3% to +0.6% of NOV, with steady-state guidance lifted towards 6%.
  • Competition has hardened: Flipkart Minutes overtook Instamart in August 2026, Amazon plans a $3B push, and inventory-led FDI risk is unresolved.

The Non-Obvious Insight

Per-store productivity — not store count — decides whether 3,000 dark stores create value.

Average NOV per store per day held at about ₹7.7 lakh in FY25 even as store count rose 147%. If tier-2 stores slip below ₹6 lakh a day, the whole network turns value-destructive; each ₹1 lakh a day of shortfall across 600 stores is worth about ₹220 Cr of NOV a year. That is the threshold I track, rather than headline order growth.

What Is Booked and What Remains

Competition and Risks

  • The field: top-10-city dark-store counts (Aug 2026) — Blinkit 969, Zepto 828, Flipkart Minutes 627, Instamart 615. National GMV share clusters at 46–52% Blinkit, 27–29% Zepto and 21–22% Instamart.
  • Regulatory: inventory-led FDI for domestic e-commerce remains closed. The hedge is a third-party-seller migration playbook for non-perishable SKUs, ready to fire if the rule tightens.
  • Capex: about ₹6,000–7,500 Cr to reach 3,000 stores — cash-feasible, but gated by per-store productivity: defer stores forecast below ₹5 lakh a day.
  • Why the plan could fail: per-store productivity slips; FDI tightens; ad take-rate stalls. Each has a named check that would catch it.

The Twelve-Month Roadmap

Run-rates are illustrative analyst estimates on a base of roughly ₹70,000–80,000 Cr FY27 NOV; the appendix shows the sensitivity (for example, 5% margin on ₹85,000 Cr of NOV is about ₹4,250 Cr). Three decisions are asked of the CEO within 60 days: a ₹1,800–2,400 Cr tier-2 capex envelope gated on per-store productivity, a 12-month programme to lift ad take-rate by 100 bps, and the FDI insurance playbook.

  • M0–2 · Mobilize: PMO under the CFO's office; baseline dashboard live; tier-1 saturation study; FDI contingency playbook drafted.
  • M2–5 · Quick wins: +150 net metro stores; ad-stack pilot; loyalty handoff with Zomato Gold; close 30–40 sub-threshold stores. About ₹150–250 Cr run-rate.
  • M5–9 · Redesign: +400 tier-2/3 stores behind the productivity gate; Hyperpure fresh SKUs; full ad-stack rollout. About ₹1,000–1,500 Cr run-rate.
  • M9–12 · Embed: About 3,000 stores; ROCE above 40% disclosed; year-two playbook signed off. About ₹4,000–5,000 Cr run-rate.

What I learned

Answer first. Writing the conclusion as one falsifiable sentence — with a number, a date and a named check — forces every slide to earn its place. The lever that matters is rarely the headline one: here it was productivity per store, not store count.
