The Brief

Snitch grew revenue 82-fold in five years — from ₹11 Cr in FY21 to ₹900 Cr in FY26 (unaudited, +80% year on year) — while staying near breakeven, with a repeat rate of about 45% and inventory turns of 45–60 days. The complication: it sells to everyone from a 21-year-old in Surat to a 33-year-old architect in Bengaluru, yet its ad spend, store roll-out and discount cadence do not yet act differently across them. The question: at ₹1,000 Cr scale, which customer do we plant our flag with, and which do we honestly defer?

Four Corners and an Empty Middle

No one owns "daily drop, design-first, premium but accessible" for the metro 22–30 man. That white space, plus Snitch's own inventory turns and repeat rate, is the case for focus.

Four Candidate Segments

Picking the Beach-Head

Four segments, scored on five axes. The Design-First Man is the only one to score a perfect 5 on four of them.

Scores are 1–5, weighted with size and brand fit highest and profit potential second. The call: concentrate paid spend on the Design-First Man for 18 months. Phase 2 (the Crisp Commuter tier) opens once his share passes 45% and repeat clears 50%; tier-2 expansion becomes its own programme only after metro NPS passes 50 and the ₹1,500–2,500 tier is profitable.

Positioning and the 4Ps

Plant the flag where no one is sitting — premium, drop-led, without licence dependency.

For style-aware men 22–30 who want distinctive but affordable everyday style, Snitch is the design-forward, daily-drop menswear brand. Unlike Bewakoof, not mass-quirky; unlike Souled Store, no licensed pop culture; unlike XYXX, basics that are drop-led. Result: a monthly wardrobe refresh.

  • Product: keep the daily-drop cadence and add a ₹1,500–2,500 premium tier, across roughly 7,000 SKUs.
  • Price: lift average order value from about ₹950 to about ₹1,150 over four quarters; cap mass promotions at 12%.
  • Place: 200+ stores by FY27, including 12 metro "drop-stores"; extend Snitch Quick to eight cities.
  • Promotion: drop-led creator narratives, 28 a quarter; cut mass-sale events.

Roadmap, Risks and Decisions

Modelled impact: ₹120–200 Cr of incremental revenue over 18 months — the low end if the brand-fit curve simply holds, the high end if the premium tier reaches 18% of orders. Three risks could break the case: price compression from ₹399 quick-commerce copycats, rivals scaling licensed drops faster than expected, and inventory turns slipping past 70 days as SKUs and stores expand. Four decisions are asked of leadership: confirm the primary segment, approve the premium tier, approve the 200+ store plan, and approve brand-led marketing.

  • Q1: Foundation: brand audit, segment lock, a 600-customer qualitative panel.
  • Q2: Product: design the first 50 SKUs of the premium tier; reset pricing and cap promotions.
  • Q3: Stores: pilot 12 metro drop-stores; grow from 115 to 180 stores.
  • Q4: Narrative: 28 creator-drops a quarter, brand-led rather than promo-led.

What I learned

Focus is a decision to disappoint other customers on purpose. Scoring segments on explicit axes makes that choice defensible, and writing down the triggers for the next phase keeps the plan honest about what it is deferring.
