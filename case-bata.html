Bata

Youth-centric brand transformation. Independent case study, not client work by Sagar Sharma.

A brand Gen Z associates with their parents. Repositioning, a new sub-brand, and a creator-led growth engine.

  • ~₹35B: Category revenue
  • +15%: Projected consideration, 16–30
  • 25%: Projected revenue from new line

The Constraint

Against Nike, Adidas and Puma in Indian footwear (~₹35B), the growth constraint was clear: weak Gen Z relevance from a "parent brand" perception, low cultural connect, and poor digital discovery.

The Consumer Reality

Gen Z carries $250B in spending power, and 58% of its discovery is influencer-driven. A brand invisible in that layer isn't competing — it's absent.

The White Space

"Affordable Cool" — under ₹4,000, combining style, sustainability and accessibility. Nobody credibly owns it.

Repositioning

From "utility footwear" to "accessible style + sustainability", through one platform line: Everyday style, responsibly made.

Product — BATA ECHO

  • A Gen Z sub-brand: retro-modern sneakers in recycled PET, bamboo fibre and bio-foam, with a circular lifecycle.
  • Priced at $60–$90 — affordable, still aspirational.
  • AR try-ons, app-based customisation, digital product passports.

Growth Engine

  • Always-on Creator Engine — 500+ micro-influencers a month.
  • Short-form video, campus drops, behind-the-scenes storytelling; an ambassador programme across 50 universities.
  • Social commerce: shoppable video, live drops, a Discord community.
  • Phygital retail: AR mirrors, smart shelves, online-to-offline journeys.

Projected Impact

How I contributed

Independent case study. I analysed the footwear market, defined the "Affordable Cool" white space, and designed the BATA ECHO sub-brand, creator engine and phygital retail plan.

What I learned

Legacy perception is a positioning problem, not a product problem. Bata needed a separate space for Gen Z rather than a facelift of the parent brand.
