Vodafone Idea (Vi)

Profitability turnaround diagnostic · Telecom. Independent case study, not client work by Sagar Sharma.

Close half the ₹69 ARPU gap to Airtel, take cost out of the network and free working capital — about ₹8,500 Cr of modelled EBITDA by FY29. A 24-month diagnostic written for a CFO office.

  • ₹69: Per-subscriber ARPU gap to Airtel
  • ~₹8,500 Cr: Base-case EBITDA uplift by FY29 (modelled)
  • 43% → 51%: Adj. EBITDA margin, FY26 to FY29 target

Independent case study — written as a note to a CFO office, not work for Vi. I built the profit tree and FY25→FY26 EBITDA bridge, benchmarked three peers, sized three levers with owners and confidence, and set a three-phase roadmap and risk register. Bridge values are derived attributions within ±10%, as the deck states.

The Question

ARPU is the lever, margin is the symptom, time is the constraint.

Vi is the third private operator, with 192.8M subscribers, a customer ARPU of ₹190 and FY26 revenue of ₹44,873 Cr. The AGR ruling and a ₹45,000 Cr three-year capex plan open a 24-month window of policy clarity. The complication: ARPU sits ₹69 a month below Airtel's ₹264, Adjusted EBITDA margin trails by about 1,700 bps, and the underlying Q1 FY27 loss was still ₹3,754 Cr. The question: how does Vi close the gap by half within 24 months, restore underlying profitability, and stay financed through the cycle?

The Non-Obvious Insight

FY26's reported profit of ₹51,970 Cr is an accounting one-off. Strip out the ₹57,491 Cr AGR derecognition and Vi's underlying Q1 FY27 loss was still ₹3,754 Cr — the case stands on operating fundamentals, not balance-sheet relief.

The Gap to Airtel

Airtel is best-in-class on ARPU, margin and coverage; Jio leads on scale; Vi leads on growth velocity. The combined signal: Vi has the room and the subscriber base — the gap is monetisation, not infrastructure. A profit tree across subscribers, ARPU, mix, opex and margin shows the gap is split between price (about ₹60 per subscriber a month), premium mix and per-subscriber cost intensity, not volume.

What the EBITDA Bridge Says

FY25 to FY26: Adjusted EBITDA up ₹876 Cr, from ₹18,127 Cr to ₹19,003 Cr. ARPU did the heavy lifting — and ARPU alone cannot close 1,700 bps.

ARPU contributed roughly ₹1,400 Cr of the year's gain, while the 5.4M subscribers lost cost about ₹400 Cr. Opex rose only ₹426 Cr against ₹1,302 Cr more revenue — real leverage, but small. So the FY27–29 lever mix has to tilt towards cost and working capital, not pricing alone.

Three Named Levers

Base case: about ₹8,500 Cr of added EBITDA and about ₹3,750 Cr of one-time cash by FY29, taking EBITDA to about ₹27,500 Cr (about ₹29,000 Cr if tariffs move) and margin to 51%. Each ₹10 of ARPU is worth roughly ₹2,300 Cr of revenue and ₹1,000 Cr of EBITDA at current margins.

The Roadmap

  • Sep ’26 – Dec ’26: Quick wins: reposition entry plans and renegotiate the top vendor contracts. ARPU ₹195 → ₹200; about ₹800–1,200 Cr run-rate.
  • Dec ’26 – Jun ’27: Build: 5G to 60% population coverage, a postpaid motion, AI-driven network operations. ARPU → ₹210; margin 46%.
  • Jul ’27 – Mar ’29: Scale: 5G to 95%+, spectrum redeployment and fibre sale-leaseback. ARPU → ₹227; margin 51%.

Risks and the Decision

  • Subscriber stabilisation reverses (medium): the first net adds in eight quarters are fragile. Test packs in four circles first; pause price actions if monthly net adds turn negative for two months.
  • Phase-2 capex overruns the ₹45,000 Cr envelope (high): stage-gate each phase with a fixed envelope; use vendor financing; pull forward sale-leaseback if the envelope breaches 110%.
  • Tower-contract renegotiation breaks down (medium): prepay four quarters to unlock a discount and line up regional fallbacks.
  • The decision asked: approve about ₹5,600 Cr of net Phase-1 and early Phase-2 spend, and authorise the CFO to lead the tower-contract renegotiation, with a 30-day checkpoint.

How I contributed

Independent case study. I built the profit tree and EBITDA bridge, benchmarked three peers, sized the three levers with owners and confidence, and set the roadmap, risk register and the decision asked.

What I learned

Read the headline number before you believe it: a ₹51,970 Cr reported profit was an accounting one-off, and the underlying loss told the real story. And a bridge shows where past gains came from — which is how you see that the next ones have to come from somewhere else.
